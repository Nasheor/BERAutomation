What the New Tool Adds Over the Excel

🤖
4-Angle AI Classification
The Excel relied on a human visually guessing from one Street View angle. The tool sends Front, Right, Rear, and Left views to Claude in a single request. Claude cross-references all four — spotting oil tanks in the back garden, heat pump units on the side, shared party walls that confirm the building type.
Excel: 1 angle, manual guess  →  Tool: 4 angles, AI classified
📐
Automatic Footprint Extraction
The Excel required manually clicking around a rooftop with the Google Maps ruler tool. The tool runs two independent automated methods — OpenCV computer vision and Claude Vision — and reconciles the results. If they agree within 30%, confidence is boosted. A terrace correction divides shared row dimensions by unit count.
Excel: manual clicks  →  Tool: OpenCV + Claude, reconciled
🔧
Retrofit Modelling
The Excel produced a single BER band for the current state. The tool adds a full retrofit analysis — wall insulation, roof insulation, window replacement, and heating system upgrade — and shows the before/after BER band comparison in one click. A pre-1980 D2 house can reach B1 with standard measures.
Excel: current state only  →  Tool: before + after retrofit
🌍
5-Country Climate Data
The Excel included Ireland, Belgium, France, Germany, and the Netherlands as country options. The tool faithfully replicates this, with all climate data (HDD, solar irradiance, heating days) per country. Selecting Germany gives near-identical results to Ireland — same HDD — while France is noticeably milder at 1,462 heating degree days vs Ireland's 2,149.
Excel: manual country selection  →  Tool: auto from geocoder (IE) or dropdown
📊
Full Energy Profile
The Excel output was a BER band and a kWh/m² number. The tool adds annual CO₂ emissions, a full heat loss breakdown (transmission vs ventilation vs solar/internal gains), the heating demand split from hot water, and an interactive BER scale with the property's band highlighted — all displayed in the Streamlit UI.
Excel: BER band + kWh/m²  →  Tool: band + CO₂ + breakdown + retrofit
🛡
Confidence-Gated Fallbacks
When Claude can't see the building clearly — hedge in the way, no Street View coverage, new development — it reports a confidence score below 0.4 and the pipeline uses safe defaults rather than propagating a bad classification. The Excel had no such safety net; a wrong guess went straight into the calculation.
Excel: wrong guess → wrong result  →  Tool: low confidence → safe defaults

The What-If Engine — Engaging Communities as Energy Citizens

🏘️
From Data to Decision
A BER band alone is abstract. The what-if model converts it into a concrete question residents understand: "What would it take to halve my heating bill?" The tool answers that question for any specific home — without an assessor visit.
⚡
Enabling Energy Citizens
Energy citizenship requires informed participation. When communities can see their own street — every house rated and modelled — they can identify which neighbours are most vulnerable, plan area-wide retrofit schemes, and present evidence to local authorities. The tool makes that information accessible to non-experts.
📍
CIRCUS Scale: Estate-Level Screening
Running the what-if model on every house in an estate of 200 homes takes minutes, not months. The output tells planners which interventions deliver the biggest gain per euro across the whole community — wall insulation or heating upgrades, and in what order. That is the planning intelligence CIRCUS partners need.
